Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Manhattan Restoration Co employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate

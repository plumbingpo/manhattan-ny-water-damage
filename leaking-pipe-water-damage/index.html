Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Manhattan

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. We serve Manhattan homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. This is where our team comes in. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether mold is present and needs containment and removal before drying can finish.
  • The amount of flooring, cabinetry, insulation or drywall that must come out and be replaced.
  • Whether the damage stayed in one room or spread to others and to the level below.
  • The length of time the leak ran and the distance moisture traveled from the pipe.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A crimp ring or solder joint that gives out inside a ceiling or wall cavity where nobody can see it.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Wet Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. Left in a sealed cavity it can stay wet for a long time, which is why we usually take it out.

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

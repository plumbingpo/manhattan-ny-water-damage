How to Prevent Storm Damage Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Homes in hurricane country benefit from shutters or impact-rated window glass
  • Cut back limbs that hang over or close to the house so wind cannot throw them into it
  • Have your roof checked annually and fix vulnerable areas ahead of the stormy months
  • Good attic insulation and ventilation stop ice dams from forming along the roof edge
  • Clogged gutters overflow in a downpour, so clear them and the downspouts often
  • Know how to shut off your main water supply in case a storm damages a pipe

When to Call for Storm Damage Repair in Manhattan

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Puddles near skylights, beside a chimney or on the attic floor
  • New brown rings or damp marks on walls and ceilings once the storm passes
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Water in the lowest level after storm drains back up or overflow
  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • Bubbling paint and soft, drooping spots overhead once the rain stops
  • Damaged or displaced flashing around roof penetrations, valleys or edges

The Benefits of Professional Storm Damage Repair

The repairs a storm leaves behind are tangled together, and sorting them out correctly calls for an experienced restoration crew. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the route water took and find all the areas it reached.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. If a tarp is fastened poorly, it can direct water where you do not want it or give out when the wind picks up.

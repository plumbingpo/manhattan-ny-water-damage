Crawl Space Water Removal: What Manhattan Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
How We Fix It

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Air From Below

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

Settling Under the House

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Contaminated Water

Do not assume the water below your floor is clean. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

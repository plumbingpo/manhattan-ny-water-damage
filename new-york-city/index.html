Found Water in Your New York City Home? Start Here

People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on New York City jobs, with the better option for each.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. It is an electric shock risk and it will ruin the machine; use towels and a mop. Leave ceiling fixtures off if the ceiling above is wet, and keep away from any part that droops. Water Damage We See Most in New York City

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones New York City property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. For roof leaks in New York City, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. We remove any standing water in New York City homes, dry the walls and slab, and explain what likely caused it.
